# Best practices

Rules for whoever writes a deck, founder or AI agent. Each rule carries a tag:
- **data**: measured;
- **expert**: practitioner advice;
- **standard**: WCAG;
- **own**: this blueprint's choice.

Break a rule when you have a reason; a check that fails publish can't be broken.

## Story

1. Open with one sentence: what you do, and for whom. *Expert:* Sequoia.
2. Make the headline the claim, not a label: "Revenue grew 18% a month", not "Traction". The headlines alone should tell the story. *Expert:* Minto, YC.
3. One point per slide, with its evidence. *Data:* assertion–evidence slides beat bullets on understanding (110 engineering students). *Expert:* TED.
4. Pick the outline:
   - investors: problem, solution, why now, market, competition, model, team, ask (*expert:* Sequoia, YC);
   - customers: the change, the stakes, the future, how, proof (*expert:* Raskin), then one next step (*own*).
5. Answer the 1–3 biggest objections early. *Expert:* Hoffman.
6. End with a concrete ask: amount, use, milestones, runway. Intercom asked $600k for 18 months. *Expert:* Point Nine.

## Evidence

7. Show traction per period, not only cumulative. Mark forecasts. Every chart needs a unit, a period and a source. *Expert:* Point Nine, Index.
8. One message per chart: highlight it and grey out the rest. *Expert:* Knaflic.
9. Size the market bottom-up: buyers × price, with a source. *Own.*
10. Show the team's proof, not their titles. *Data:* in an AngelList experiment, investors responded to team information, not to traction or existing investors.
11. Keep the caveats, in `detail` or the appendix. *Expert:* Amazon's six-page memos.

## Length and reading

12. Keep it short. *Data, expert:*
    - successful pre-seed decks averaged 16 slides, failed ones 19 (a correlation);
    - Index advises about 12, plus an appendix;
    - Airbnb had 14, Buffer 13, Intercom 8.
13. Every slide must stand alone and work on a phone, because reads are short and often on a phone. *Data:*
    - DocSend measured 3:44 per successful deck (200 companies), and under 2 minutes at seed in 2023;
    - about a third of decks are opened on a phone (vendor data).
14. Presenting is not reading: don't put the speaker's words on the slide. *Data:* in Mayer's teaching studies, duplicate text hurt learning in 16 of 16 tests. *Expert:* Duarte. So:
    - the headline and visual appear everywhere;
    - `detail` is for readers;
    - `notes` are for the speaker.
15. No decoration. *Data:* Mayer's coherence principle held in 23 of 23 tests (teaching).
16. Plan the seconds per slide and rehearse, at 100–165 words a minute (Speaker Coach). *Expert.*

## Design

17. Headlines ≥ 48 px and body ≥ 28 px on a 1280×720 canvas. *Own;* TED asks for ≥ 42 pt on stage.
18. Contrast: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text and chart parts. Never rely on colour alone. *Standard.*
19. No animation. *Own;* WCAG covers motion at AAA.
20. Give every image alt text. On a product slide, show the steps, not raw screenshots. *Expert:* Hale.

## Myths

- **"Exactly 10 slides" or "3 points":** habits, not proven rules.
- **"Famous decks are the formula":** the 2004 Facebook deck sold ads; Coinbase's ask was in the email, not the deck.
- **"Long reading means interest":** in 2023, traction slides of decks that didn't raise got 110% more time than in 2022. More time can mean doubt.
- **"AI makes a deck investor-ready":** there is no evidence for this.

## For AI agents

- Use only claims and numbers the founder gave, and source every number.
- Never invent traction, customers or quotes; leave the field empty and say so.
- Give the founder the headline outline to check.
